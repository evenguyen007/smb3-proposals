Sample 1 · Carousel

Things your OBGYN might say that mean you need a new OBGYN.

Slide 1 · Cover
Things your OBGYN might say that mean you need a new OBGYN.
Visual: Warm cream backdrop. Title in bold serif. 💛 in the corner. No other elements.
Slide 2
"Painful periods are normal."
Painful is not the same as debilitating. If your period stops you from working, walking, eating, or sleeping — that is not a "normal period." That is a symptom. Save this. Show it to the next doctor who tries this line on you.
Slide 3
"You're too young to have endometriosis."
Endometriosis has been documented in patients as young as 8. The average diagnostic delay is still 7-10 years. "Too young" is the phrase that costs people their fertility, their organs, and their twenties. 🫠
Slide 4
"Just go on birth control and see if it helps."
Hormonal suppression can manage symptoms for some people. It does not treat endometriosis. The disease is still there, still spreading, still planting nerve fibers and growing extracellular vesicles. "See if it helps" is not a treatment plan — it's a stalling tactic.
Slide 5
"Pregnancy will cure your endo."
It will not. Endometriosis is not cured by anything except, possibly, expert excision — and even then, central sensitization can persist. Telling a sick patient to get pregnant as a treatment is medical malpractice with a smile.
Slide 6
"I can do your excision surgery — I do gyn surgeries all the time."
A general OBGYN doing endo surgery is not the same as a confirmed endometriosis excision specialist doing endo surgery. The disease is complex, systemic, and often missed by surgeons who don't specialize in it. Many patients leave general-OBGYN excisions worse than they started. Ask for a confirmed excision specialist. Always.
Slide 7
"Your imaging is clean — there's nothing there."
Imaging cannot reliably detect endometriosis. Most lesions are too flat, too superficial, or in places imaging doesn't see well. A "clean MRI" or "normal ultrasound" does not rule out endo. Your symptoms still count.
