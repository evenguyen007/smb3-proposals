Sample 01 — South Asian Niche Reel
What Gets Passed Down
Caption
This one is for anyone who grew up in a house where love was something you felt but no one really said. And then spent years wondering why setting any kind of limit felt like a betrayal.
Script
Your parents didn't withhold love. They gave you the only version of love they had been given. Sacrifice as language. Presence as protection. A packed lunch, a fixed computer, a flight booked without you asking. But when love and compliance live in the same house, the body learns something that doesn't disappear when you move out. That making space for yourself means taking something away from someone else. That being too much, too loud, too present, is a risk. That the safest version of you is the one who disappears a little. This isn't a personality trait. It's a nervous system that learned very early how to keep connection alive. And it's one of the most common things I see in the room.
This is written to land in the South Asian community without explaining it to outsiders. The reference points (compliance as connection, disappearing to keep the peace) name something specific enough to feel recognized. The last line pulls the viewer toward therapy without pitching it.
Sample 02 — Emotional Presence Reel
The Version That Holds It Together
Caption
Writing this one because I keep meeting people who are quietly exhausted and have no idea that's allowed to matter.
Script
The version of you that holds everything together is not the strongest version of you. It's the one that learned, very early, that falling apart wasn't an option. Maybe because someone needed you to be okay. Maybe because showing emotion felt dangerous. Maybe because you were the one people leaned on, and you leaned on no one. So you built something efficient. A version of yourself that functions. That handles things. That can be counted on. What doesn't get named is what it costs. How little space it leaves for what you actually feel. How the exhaustion isn't just physical. It's what happens when you've been your own floor for a very long time. Therapy isn't about dismantling that version of you. It's about building someone that version can finally rest inside.
This uses the same formula as your February reel: internal state named first, clinical framing held until the reader is already inside the feeling. The last two lines mirror your "unwind your brain and show up as your authentic self" positioning without quoting it. This is the formula. Two scripts per month in this register is a strategy.
