6-slide carousel · for @therapy.with.emilee

Slide 01 · Cover

why you can't
just "start saying no"
(it's not willpower)

Slide 02 · The scene

a client sat down last week and said something i hear constantly:

"i said yes again. i don't even know why. i was exhausted. i didn't want to go. i said yes anyway."

she wasn't being a pushover. she wasn't "bad at boundaries." something else was happening — and once she understood it, the whole pattern finally made sense.

Slide 03 · The real reason

people-pleasing is not a personality trait.

it is a nervous system response.

at some point, saying yes kept you safe. saying no felt like a risk — losing someone's approval, triggering someone's anger, becoming "too much" or "not enough."

your brain learned: yes = safe. no = danger.

and brains that learn something is dangerous do not unlearn it just because you decided to try harder. 🤍

Slide 04 · What it actually feels like

here is what that looks like in real life:

  • you say yes and immediately feel resentful
  • you rehearse the "no" a hundred times and still can't say it out loud
  • you feel physically anxious when someone seems disappointed in you
  • you apologize for things that aren't your fault
  • you feel relief when plans get cancelled — because then you didn't have to cancel them yourself

this is not a character flaw. it is a very logical response to something that happened.

Slide 05 · What actually helps

so what do you do with this?

you don't just "start saying no." you start by understanding what the yes has been protecting.

when my client figured out that her yes was keeping a very old version of herself safe — the one who grew up in a house where disapproval felt genuinely threatening — everything shifted.

she didn't need a script. she needed to understand that the current moment was different from the one she was protecting herself from.

that's the work. 🤍

you just haven't had someone help you understand the why yet.
